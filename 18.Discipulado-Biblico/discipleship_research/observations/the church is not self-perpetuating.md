# The Church Is Not Self-Perpetuating

Buildings remain after builders die.

Institutions can outlive their founders.

But households are different.

Bodies are different.

Flocks are different.

Brides are different.

They require life.

And life must continue.

# The Church Is Composed Of Believers

Not descendants.

Not citizens.

Not ethnic groups.

Not members by birth.

Every generation enters by faith.

That means the church cannot simply preserve itself biologically.

It must continually receive new life.

---

# Therefore, The Church Is Generational

This is fascinating.

Because Christ is building something that must continue from generation to generation.

That means the Builder's work necessarily involves:

```
Believers↓Future believers↓Future believers↓Future believers
```

Otherwise the house dies.

# Suddenly 2 Timothy 2:2 Looks Different

```
Paul↓Timothy↓Faithful men↓Others
```

Notice the generational character.

Not merely succession of office.

Transmission.

Continuation.

---

# Acts 2

Three thousand.

Then more.

Then more.

Then more.

The life continues.

---

# Ephesians 4

Until we all attain...

This process spans generations.

The body outlives individual members.

---

# An Interesting Thought

If the church is a household, then households naturally think generationally.

Parents think:

> Who will carry this after us?

Builders think:

> Will this endure?

---

# This Raises A Huge Question

Suppose Christ's intended product is:

```
A mature peopleconformed to Christprepared as His bride
```

Then how does He ensure that the next generation participates?

This is where:

- Matthew 28
- Acts 14
- Ephesians 4
- 2 Timothy 2

may suddenly converge.

Because if one generation does not participate in the Builder's work, the visible expression of the house in that place dies.
