# Ultimate Goal

and

# Present Building Objective

Those are not necessarily the same thing.

---

## Ultimate Goal

God's purposes may indeed encompass things larger than the church:

- Kingdom
- Israel
- Nations
- Restoration
- New Creation
- Summing up all things in Christ
- Bride
- Fullness of times

Those are enormous categories.

But if we ask:

> What is Christ explicitly said to be building right now?

There is only one explicit statement:

### Matthew 16:18

> I will build My church.

Not:

> I will build My kingdom.

Not:

> I will build Israel.

Not:

> I will build all things.

Those things may belong to larger purposes, but the explicit object of the verb "build" is:

```
My church
```

That observation alone is important.

---

# Builders Think In Stages

Suppose I build a house.

My ultimate goal might be:

```
A household
```

But my current objective is:

```
Foundation
```

Those are not contradictory.

Likewise:

```
Ultimate Purpose↓Present Work↓Immediate Means
```

need not be identical.

---

# Ephesians 4 Gives An "Until"

This is fascinating.

Christ gives gifts

↓

Saints equipped

↓

Body built up

↓

Until...

- unity of faith
- knowledge of the Son
- mature man
- fullness of Christ

Notice something.

Paul is not talking about the kingdom.

Nor the restoration of Israel.

Nor the new heavens and earth.

His concern is the body.

The church.

Its growth.

Its maturity.

Its fullness.

That seems to be a present objective.

---

# Matthew 28 Also Has An "Until"

> I am with you always, until the end of the age.

Interesting.

The Builder remains with His workers until a certain point.

That implies an ongoing project.

---

# Romans 11 Has Another "Until"

> until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in.

Different subject.

Different concern.

Different "until."

Therefore, we should be careful not to collapse all the "untils" into one.

They may harmonize without being identical.

# This May Become One Of The Most Important Principles In The Manual

## El propósito final y la obra presente no son necesariamente idénticos

Los constructores trabajan con objetivos inmediatos y con propósitos más amplios.

De la misma manera, las Escrituras presentan una obra presente y una consumación futura.

Jesús declaró:

> "Yo edificaré Mi iglesia."

Por lo tanto, la iglesia constituye el objeto explícito de Su obra de edificación.

Esto no significa que el propósito final de Dios se limite a la iglesia.

Las Escrituras también hablan del reino, de Israel, de las naciones, y de la restauración de todas las cosas.

Sin embargo, no debemos confundir el propósito final con la obra que Cristo afirma estar realizando actualmente.

Comprender correctamente la obra presente del Constructor es esencial, porque todo ministerio, todo servicio y toda participación deben armonizar con aquello que Cristo está edificando hoy.

And I think this brings us to perhaps the most important question we have yet asked:

> **What does a completed church look like?**

Not the eternal kingdom.

Not the new creation.

But the object Christ explicitly says He is building.

Because if we can answer that, then perhaps we can finally understand:

- discipleship,
- leadership,
- church life,
- gifts,
- generational transmission,

as means serving that present objective of the Builder.
