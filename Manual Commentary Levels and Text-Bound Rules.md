# Manual Commentary Levels and Text-Bound Rules

## Purpose

This document exists to establish clear boundaries for commentary, clarification, explanation, and synthesis within the manuals.

The goal of the project is not to eliminate all explanation.

If manuals only repeated the biblical text exactly as written, the manuals themselves would become unnecessary.

The purpose of the manuals is to:

- clarify,
- slow down the reading process,
- expose textual relationships,
- explain visible flow,
- restate carefully,
- and guide the reader through the passage.

However, this must happen in a controlled and transparent way.

The problem is not explanation itself.

The problem is invisible, uncontrolled, or imported interpretation.

Therefore, commentary must remain:

- text-bound,
- traceable,
- observable,
- and reproducible from the text itself.

## The Core Principle

The manuals are not attempting to produce:

- systematic theology,
- speculative psychology,
- doctrinal imposition,
- or uncontrolled abstraction.

The manuals are attempting to explain and expose what can visibly be observed from:

- wording,
- grammar,
- connectors,
- contrasts,
- repetitions,
- structure,
- immediate context,
- lexical meaning,
- rhetorical movement,
- and argument flow.

The reader should be able to visibly trace how a comment emerged from the text.

## Important Clarification

The distinction is not:

- “interpretation vs no interpretation.”

That distinction is too simplistic.

Every explanation, clarification, lexical definition, or structural observation already involves some level of interpretation.

The real issue is:

- what kind of interpretation is occurring,
- how far it moves from the text,
- whether the movement is visible,
- and whether it remains controlled by the text.

## Levels of Commentary

## L0 — Direct Text

The text is quoted directly with no restatement.

Example:

> “No se priven el uno del otro.”

This level contains no explanatory commentary.

## L1 — Lexical Clarification

A word, phrase, or idiom is clarified directly from its immediate meaning.

Example:

> “La expresión apunta a abstenerse de relación sexual.”

This level is allowed when:

- the clarification is directly tied to the expression itself,
- the context supports the meaning,
- and no external theology is introduced.

## L2 — Immediate Contextual Clarification

The text is restated or clarified directly from the immediate wording and context.

Example:

> “La abstinencia dentro del matrimonio no debe ser impuesta unilateralmente.”

This is acceptable because:

- “el uno del otro,”
- “de común acuerdo,”
- and the reciprocal structure
  all visibly support the observation.

This level explains what is already visibly present in the text.

## L3 — Structural or Flow Observation

The commentary explains movement, contrast, progression, or rhetorical structure.

Example:

> “Pablo vuelve a mover la atención hacia el hermano.”

This level is acceptable when:

- the textual movement is visible,
- the contrast or transition is observable,
- and the observation remains anchored to the actual flow of the passage.

This level must avoid excessive abstraction.

## L4 — Controlled Synthesis

Multiple observations are combined into a broader conclusion.

Example:

> “Pablo está corrigiendo una perspectiva individualista acerca de la libertad.”

This level is more dangerous because:

- abstraction increases,
- compression increases,
- and distance from explicit wording increases.

This level is only acceptable when:

- the synthesis is demonstrably traceable,
- the supporting observations are visible,
- and the conclusion remains textually constrained.

## L5 — Theological System Building

This level begins constructing doctrinal systems beyond the immediate textual flow.

Examples:

- importing theological frameworks,
- harmonizing large doctrinal systems,
- systematic theological conclusions,
- denominational synthesis.

This level is restricted.

The manuals are not intended to function primarily as systematic theology.

## L6 — Speculative Psychology or Motives

This level assigns internal motives, emotions, or intentions not established by the text.

Examples:

- “Pablo was secretly frustrated,”
- “The Corinthians were jealous because...,”
- “They emotionally feared...,”
- speculative emotional reconstruction.

This level is not allowed unless the text explicitly establishes it.

## L7 — External Doctrinal Imposition

This level imports conclusions from outside systems and places them onto the text.

Examples:

- forcing theological traditions onto passages,
- reading denominational systems into the text,
- forcing later theological categories onto the argument.

This level is not allowed.

## Commentary Must Show Its Basis

A central principle of the manuals is that comments should visibly demonstrate how they were reached.

The manuals should not present conclusions as if they appeared automatically or intuitively.

Observations and explanations should expose the basis behind the statement whenever possible.

This may include:

- morphology,
- grammar,
- lexical meaning,
- immediate context,
- historical context,
- scriptural context,
- connector relationships,
- contrasts,
- repeated wording,
- rhetorical structure,
- or argument flow.

The goal is not merely to state conclusions.

The goal is to help the reader visibly follow how the conclusion emerged from the text.

A good manual does not merely assert.

A good manual exposes the path from text to observation.

This is especially important in difficult or easily misunderstood passages.

## Use of H6 and Bullet Point Expansion

The manuals should make intentional use of deeper explanatory levels beneath the primary commentary.

Especially important are:

- H6 sections,
- and bullet points beneath H6.

These layers exist to:

- slow down difficult passages,
- expose reasoning carefully,
- clarify complex relationships,
- explain contextual factors,
- unfold implications gradually,
- and make difficult material easier to follow.

The purpose of these lower levels is not merely to add extra information.

The purpose is to visibly expose how conclusions are being reached.

This means the manual should not simply say:

- what is being concluded,

but also:

- why,
- from where,
- and based on what textual or contextual observations.

These explanatory levels may draw from:

- morphology,
- grammar,
- lexical meaning,
- immediate context,
- historical context,
- scriptural context,
- repeated wording,
- rhetorical structure,
- connector relationships,
- argument flow,
- or visible patterns inside the passage.

The key principle is transparency.

The reader should be able to follow the reasoning process step-by-step.

This is especially important in passages involving:

- marriage,
- sexuality,
- freedom,
- conscience,
- spiritual gifts,
- historical practices,
- rhetorical arguments,
- or places where readers commonly misunderstand the flow.

The higher heading levels should preserve the main flow of the passage.

The lower levels allow the manual to patiently expand and clarify without overcrowding the main structure.

This is extremely important in passages such as 1 Corinthians 7, where:

- Paul moves carefully between commands,
- concessions,
- practical concerns,
- present circumstances,
- personal judgment,
- and broader principles.

The use of H6 and bullet expansion allows the manual to:

- patiently unfold the passage,
- expose the reasoning behind conclusions,
- avoid oversimplification,
- and guide the reader through the logic and flow of the passage more transparently.

## Commentary Must Remain Transparent

A key principle of the manuals is transparency.

Comments should expose how they emerged from the text.

The danger is not simply “wrong interpretation.”

The greater danger is hidden interpretation:

- invisible jumps,
- compressed assumptions,
- unexplained conclusions,
- and imported ideas that sound natural but are not visibly grounded.

Therefore:

- observations should remain traceable,
- the textual anchors should remain visible,
- and the reader should be able to follow the reasoning process.

## The Goal of the Manuals

The manuals exist to:

- expose the text,
- clarify the text,
- slow down the text,
- and guide the reader through the visible flow of the text.

The manuals are not attempting to replace Scripture.

They are attempting to help readers observe Scripture more carefully.

## Final Principle

The safest commentary is commentary where:

- the reader can visibly trace the statement back to the text,
- the movement from text to comment is transparent,
- and the explanation remains constrained by the wording and flow of the passage itself.

The farther commentary moves away from visible textual grounding, the greater the danger of uncontrolled interpretation.
