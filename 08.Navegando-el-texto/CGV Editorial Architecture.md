# CGV Editorial Architecture

## Philosophy

The CGV manual is built upon two distinct layers:

1. **The Inspired Text** (objective)
2. **Editorial Navigation** (reader assistance)

These layers must never be confused.

The inspired text determines the structure of the manual.

The editorial layer exists only to help the reader follow the author's development. It is never intended to replace, improve, or reorganize Scripture.

---

# Layer 1 — The Inspired Text

The inspired text is the permanent foundation of the manual.

Everything begins with the text itself.

## H4 — Exact Independent Clause

The H4 is the fundamental structural unit of every CGV manual.

Each H4 consists of one exact independent clause from the biblical text.

The H4 is never paraphrased.

The H4 is never rewritten.

The H4 is never chosen because it appears important.

It exists because it is an independent clause in the inspired text.

Every independent clause must become an H4.

---

## Material Attached to an H4

Every element that depends upon an independent clause belongs beneath its H4.

Examples include:

- Dependent clauses
- Participles
- Infinitives
- Relative clauses
- Purpose clauses
- Reason clauses
- Condition clauses
- Temporal clauses
- Comparisons
- Quotations
- Explanatory phrases

Nothing inspired is omitted.

Every part of Scripture must be accounted for somewhere within the manual.

---

# Layer 2 — Editorial Navigation

The editorial layer exists solely to help the reader navigate the inspired text.

These headings are not part of the biblical structure.

They are not theological divisions.

They are contextual cues created after observing the text.

Because they are editorial, they may improve over time without changing the underlying structure.

---

## H3 — Section Navigation

Each H3 belongs to one H4.

Purpose:

Help the reader recognize what this section is about.

The H3 should be:

- Short
- Clear
- Reader-oriented
- Non-theological
- Non-preachy
- Faithful to the immediate development

The H3 never replaces the H4.

---

## H2 — Development Navigation

An H2 groups several consecutive H3 sections.

Purpose:

Help the reader observe the author's current development.

An H2 does not define theology.

An H2 simply helps the reader keep track of where the author has moved.

Each H2 concludes with:

### En síntesis

This is the first place where the completed development may be summarized.

The summary should describe what the author has developed, not what the editor wishes to emphasize.

---

## H1 — Major Movement Navigation

An H1 groups several H2 developments.

Purpose:

Help the reader follow the major movements of the book.

Most books will contain only a few H1 headings.

These are navigation markers, not inspired divisions.

---

# Title and Subtitle

The title and subtitle stand above the entire navigation system.

Their purpose is to express the overall movement of the entire biblical book.

They are determined only after the entire manual has been completed.

# Editorial Principles

## 1. Structure Before Writing

The skeleton is verified and locked before editorial writing begins.

The manual is built from the skeleton.

---

## 2. Every Scripture Must Be Accounted For

Every inspired word must appear somewhere within the manual.

No clause is omitted.

No verse is ignored.

No quotation is considered unimportant.

If the Holy Spirit inspired it, the manual helps the student observe it.

---

## 3. Observation Before Conclusion

Conclusions must arise from observable evidence.

Evidence includes:

- Grammar
- Clause relationships
- Connectors
- Repetition
- Subjects
- Objects
- Pressure development
- Discourse movement

---

## 4. Greek Serves Observation

Greek is included to help students observe the text.

It is never included merely to teach grammar.

Repeated grammatical explanations should be concise and point to the appendix through footnotes.

Unique observations that enrich understanding should remain within the body of the manual.

---

## 5. The Author Controls the Pace

The manual follows the author's development.

It never resolves pressure before the author resolves it.

It never summarizes before the development is complete.

It allows the reader to experience the progression of the text.

---

## 6. Navigation Is Not Structure

H1, H2, and H3 are editorial navigation.

H4 and its attached material constitute the textual structure.

The manual distinguishes these two layers so that the inspired organization of Scripture is never replaced by editorial organization.

---

# Editorial Goal

The purpose of a CGV manual is not to tell the reader what to think about the text.

Its purpose is to help the reader carefully observe everything the inspired author wrote, in the same order and with the same development in which it was written.

The manual seeks to accompany the inspired author's movement rather than replace it with the editor's own outline.
