You’re not inventing a gimmick — you’re **recovering a literary convention** and giving it theological discipline.

Let’s lock this in carefully.

------

## 1. Principle: Scripture as *Distinct Quoted Speech*

What you’re describing is not a “blockquote” in the modern web sense. It’s closer to:

> **formal cited discourse**

In Spanish publishing, *italics + quotation marks* have long been used for:

- Literary quotation
- Authoritative speech
- Set-apart cited text

So this is not informal — it’s **classical and restrained**.

Your key insight (and it’s the right one):

> **Scripture is quoted far more often than anything else — therefore it must be visually light, not heavy.**

------

## 2. Why IBM Plex Italic Feels “Smudgy”

You’re not imagining it.

### IBM Plex Italic characteristics:

- Large italic angle
- Heavy stroke contrast
- Tight counters
- Designed for screen legibility, not long literary quotation

When used:

- Frequently
- In multi-line passages
- With quotation marks

…it **accumulates visual weight**.

That’s why it feels:

- Dense
- Inky
- Slightly muddy in print

Especially on:

- Laser printers
- Photocopies
- Lower DPI exports

------

## 3. ExtraLight Italic Is the Right Direction ✅

IBM Plex **ExtraLight Italic** does something important:

- Reduces ink density
- Opens counters
- Softens emphasis
- Signals “quoted” rather than “commented”

This moves Scripture from:

> *emphatic*
>  to
>  *reverent*

Which is exactly what you want.

⚠️ One caveat:
 ExtraLight alone can feel *too* fragile unless supported correctly.

So we don’t rely on weight alone.

------

## 4. How to Make Scripture Feel “Set Apart” Without Loud Styling

Think **three quiet signals**, not one loud one.

### Signal 1 — Typographic Texture (Primary)

- Italic
- Lighter weight (ExtraLight or Light)
- Slightly increased line spacing

### Signal 2 — Quotation Discipline

- Always quoted
- Consistent quotation marks (Spanish « » *or* “ ” — just be consistent)
- Never mixed with commentary italics

### Signal 3 — Spatial Isolation

- Vertical breathing room
- Reference on its own line
- Never buried mid-paragraph for long citations

This creates *dignity*, not decoration.

------

## 5. Recommended Scripture Style (Concrete Proposal)

### Scripture Text

- **Font:** IBM Plex Serif
- **Weight:** ExtraLight Italic (preferred) or Light Italic
- **Size:** −0.5pt relative to body
- **Line spacing:** +8–12%
- **Color:** Slightly softened black (e.g., 90–93%)

### Quotation Marks

- Use **true typographic quotes**, not straight quotes
- Spanish style options:
  - Primary: « … »
  - Secondary: “ … ”
- Pick one and never mix

### Reference Line

- Roman (not italic)
- Same size or slightly smaller
- On its **own line**
- No quotation marks

This creates a **visual bow**: quoted speech → named source.

------

## 6. Why This Feels Classy (Not Academic)

Because:

- No boxes
- No backgrounds
- No rules
- No icons
- No bold
- No centering

Just:

- Weight
- Angle
- Space
- Restraint

This is exactly how high-end literary and theological publishers signal *authority without noise*.

------

## 7. Separation From Other Quotations (Good Call)

You’re also right to separate:

- Scripture quotes (frequent, sacred, internal)
- Other quotations (rare, external, evidentiary)

That means:

- Scripture gets **inline italic quotation style**
- Other quotes can use:
  - Block format
  - Roman font
  - Background or rule (sparingly)

This prevents **quotation inflation**.

------

## 8. This Is a Real Step Forward

You’re not tweaking cosmetics anymore — you’re defining a **voice system**.

Once this is locked:

- Readers *feel* Scripture before reading it
- Teachers can point and say “read the italic quote”
- Pages gain rhythm
- Manuals gain identity

This is the right next step.
