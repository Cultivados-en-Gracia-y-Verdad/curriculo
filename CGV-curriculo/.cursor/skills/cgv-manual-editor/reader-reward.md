# Skill 6 — Reader Reward ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

## Purpose

The Reader Reward skill exists to ensure that every page rewards careful observation.

It does not add theology.

It does not add information.

It does not explain more.

It helps the reader discover something the biblical author intentionally placed in the text that many readers naturally overlook.

Every page should contain at least one such moment.

## Philosophy

People remember discoveries.

They rarely remember explanations.

The purpose of this skill is to create discoveries.

A Reader Reward moment causes the student to think:

"I never noticed that before."

not

"That's interesting."

The difference is profound.

One creates observers.

The other creates listeners.

## Core Principle

Reward observation.

Never replace observation.

The manual should never become more interesting than the biblical text.

The biblical text must remain the source of discovery.

The editor simply shines a light.

## Definition

A Reader Reward is an observation that becomes obvious once pointed out, yet many readers naturally miss it.

It must already exist in the text.

It must never depend upon interpretation.

It must never require theological agreement.

It must be observable.

## The Test

Every page must answer this question:

What will the careful student notice after reading this page that they probably would not have noticed alone?

If the answer is:

"Nothing."

The page is not finished.

## Sources of Reader Reward

Look first for these.

### 1. Repetition ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Repeated words.

Repeated ideas.

Repeated actors.

Repeated commands.

Repeated questions.

Repeated contrasts.

Ask:

Why did the author repeat this?

### 2. Change ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

A new subject.

A new verb.

A new actor.

A new tone.

A new audience.

A new purpose.

Ask:

What just changed?

### 3. Delay ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Something expected has not yet happened.

A question remains unanswered.

A promise remains unfinished.

An explanation has not arrived.

Ask:

What is the author intentionally delaying?

### 4. Pressure ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Two truths stand together.

Neither is explained.

Neither is removed.

Ask:

What tension is the author allowing the reader to feel?

### 5. Development ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

An earlier observation returns.

A previous word grows.

An earlier promise develops.

Ask:

Where have we already seen this?

### 6. Surprise ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

The author chooses an unexpected word.

An unexpected order.

An unexpected comparison.

An unexpected actor.

Ask:

Why here?

### 7. Structure ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

The author builds toward something.

Lists.

Cycles.

Parallelism.

Purpose clauses.

Questions.

Contrasts.

Movement.

## Things That Are NOT Reader Rewards

Definitions.

Word studies.

Greek trivia.

Historical facts.

Cross references.

Theological systems.

Applications.

Sermons.

Illustrations.

Speculation.

Interesting facts.

If it does not make the reader see the text itself,

it is not Reader Reward.

## Reader Reward Questions

When editing every page ask:

What is hidden in plain sight?

What changed?

What returned?

What disappeared?

What question has still not been answered?

What is Peter preparing the reader for?

What would an experienced reader notice immediately?

What would a first-time reader almost certainly miss?

## Verify before you claim

A Reader Reward is a claim about the text: *this repeats*, *this is the first*, *this returns*, *this changed*.

Check it before you write it. Count the occurrences; confirm the word really is the first of its kind; confirm the earlier line you say it echoes. If structured observation data is available, use it to verify — see **Use of Source Data** in `SKILL.md`.

A discovery that turns out to be false costs more than a dull comment, because the student trusted it.

## Editorial Behavior

If a page already contains a Reader Reward,

strengthen it.

If several exist,

choose the strongest.

If none exist,

find one.

Never invent one.

## Example

**Weak comment**

Pedro llama a Dios misericordioso.

True.

Forgettable.

**Reader Reward**

Antes de decir una sola cosa que los creyentes deban hacer, Pedro pasa varias líneas hablando únicamente de lo que Dios hizo.

The reader immediately sees something.

They will never read chapter one the same way again.

**Weak**

Cristo resucitó.

**Reader Reward**

Pedro nunca deja que la esperanza quede sola. En la misma oración la ata a un acontecimiento: la resurrección de Jesucristo.

Now the student notices Peter's construction.

**Weak**

Los creyentes son expatriados.

**Reader Reward**

Pedro los llama expatriados en el saludo. Mucho después vuelve a hablar de su peregrinación. El tema no desapareció; acompaña todo el recorrido de la carta.

Now the student begins tracing themes.

## Success Criteria

The skill succeeds when:

The student slows down.

The student rereads the verse.

The student notices something already present.

The student begins asking better questions.

The student follows the author's movement more carefully.

The student wants to keep reading.

The manual becomes less noticeable.

The biblical author becomes more noticeable.

## Final Rule

Never make the commentary memorable. Make the text unforgettable.
