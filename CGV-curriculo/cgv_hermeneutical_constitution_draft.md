# 1 Purpose and Non‑Negotiables
## 1.1 Purpose
### 1.1.1 This document exists to prevent drift from Bible‑sourced teaching into theology‑sourced teaching.
### 1.1.2 The goal is not suspicion of teachers but structural protection through repeatable guardrails.
## 1.2 Non‑Negotiables
### 1.2.1 Scripture is the highest authority in content and method.
### 1.2.2 Claims must be demonstrably anchored in the biblical text under study.
### 1.2.3 Observation precedes framing; framing never substitutes for observation.
### 1.2.4 When tensions remain in the text, we may name them but must not prematurely resolve them.

# 2 Definitions
## 2.1 Bible‑sourced teaching
### 2.1.1 Teaching where the main claims can be pointed to in the passage itself (words, grammar, discourse moves, development shifts).
## 2.2 Theology‑sourced teaching
### 2.2.1 Teaching where the main claims depend on imported categories, harmonizations, or conclusions not demanded by the passage.
## 2.3 Drift
### 2.3.1 The gradual replacement of textual demonstration with assumed frameworks.

# 3 Layered Workflow
## 3.1 Layer 1 — Textual Observation (Required)
### 3.1.1 What the text says: wording, structure, repeated terms, contrasts, connectives.
### 3.1.2 What the grammar is doing (where applicable): tense/aspect, mood, case relations, particles.
### 3.1.3 What the author is doing: purpose/move *as evidenced by textual markers*.
## 3.2 Layer 2 — Internal Coherence (Allowed, Controlled)
### 3.2.1 How the paragraph functions inside the immediate context (the surrounding flow).
### 3.2.2 How the author develops the argument across the section.
### 3.2.3 Limits: coherence claims must cite the nearest textual controls (connectives, summaries, re‑statements).
## 3.3 Layer 3 — Theological Implication (Optional, Quarantined)
### 3.3.1 Implications may be stated only after Layers 1–2 are complete.
### 3.3.2 Implications must be explicitly labeled as implications.
### 3.3.3 No implication may be taught as if it were the same thing as Layer 1 observation.

# 4 Heading Discipline
## 4.1 H1 — Macro “Doing” of the author (Experimental)
### 4.1.1 H1 must be derived from the author’s discourse action and must be defensible from textual signals.
### 4.1.2 H1 must not be a doctrinal conclusion.
### 4.1.3 Stabilizing question: If the H1 were removed, would the paragraph still force the same reading?
### 4.1.4 H1 is limited to H1 headers only (not applied as a method to H2–H6 at this stage).
## 4.2 H2 — Paragraph information (Descriptive)
### 4.2.1 H2 describes what the paragraph covers, not what we think it means.
### 4.2.2 H2 should be bottom‑shelf: concrete, plain, and text‑traceable.
## 4.3 H3–H6 — Text‑bound micro layers
### 4.3.1 H3–H6 must remain anchored to the words and development shifts of the text.
### 4.3.2 H3–H6 should “remember” H1 without being forced into it.

# 5 Drift Tests (Required Audit)
## 5.1 Imported terminology test
### 5.1.1 Did we introduce key terms not present (or not demanded) by the passage?
## 5.2 Premature resolution test
### 5.2.1 Did we resolve a tension the text leaves unresolved?
## 5.3 Harmonization test
### 5.3.1 Did we appeal to another passage to decide meaning before finishing observation here?
## 5.4 Category compression test
### 5.4.1 Did we compress a development into a system category that the text itself does not state?
## 5.5 Demonstration test
### 5.5.1 Can every major claim be pointed to in the passage (wording/grammar/discourse markers)?

# 6 Governance and Review
## 6.1 Review expectation
### 6.1.1 Every published lesson should pass the Drift Tests.
### 6.1.2 A second reader should be able to trace claims back to the text without needing the teacher’s theological background.
## 6.2 Versioning
### 6.2.1 This constitution is a living document; changes must be logged with date, rationale, and examples.
## 6.3 Experimental methods
### 6.3.1 Any experimental method (including H1 speech‑act framing) must list strengths, weaknesses, and known failure modes.
### 6.3.2 Experimental methods may be limited in scope until validated by repeated use.
