# CGV INTERNAL – STRUCTURE FOUNDATION

## Purpose

To reliably identify sentence structure before attempting interpretation.

This phase trains structural clarity, not theological insight.

------

# Layer 1 – Clause Control

### Goal

Identify subordinated clauses and anchor clauses with consistency across genres.

------

## Step 1 – Identify Subordinators

Markers that introduce subordinate clauses:

- if
- when
- while
- because
- although
- since
- after
- before
- that (content clause)
- who / which / that (relative clause)
- what (indirect question clause)

If a clause begins with one of these, it is subordinated.

Everything under that marker stays under it until the clause ends.

------

## Step 2 – Identify Anchor Clauses

After removing subordinated units, what remains are anchor clauses.

Anchor clause = independent clause not introduced by a subordinator.

Important:

Anchor ≠ theological center
 Anchor ≠ most important idea

Anchor = structural controller.

------

## Step 3 – Recognize Coordination

Words like:

- and
- but
- or

Do NOT subordinate.

They join equal clauses.

A sentence may have:

- one anchor clause
- two coordinated anchor clauses
- several anchor verbs inside one clause

Do not assume one anchor per sentence.

------

# Layer 2 – Anchor Sequence Mapping

Once clause control is stable:

1. Write out the anchor clause(s) of each sentence.
2. Read only the anchors in sequence.
3. Identify the functional move of each sentence.

Possible sentence-level functions:

- Command
- Assertion
- Evaluation
- Comparison
- Illustration
- Explanation
- Promise
- Warning
- Question
- Conclusion

Do not interpret content yet.

Just identify the rhetorical move.

------

# Layer 3 – Paragraph Movement

Read the anchor clauses in order.

Observe pattern of moves:

Examples:

- Command → Illustration → Consequence
- Assertion → Grounding → Result
- Condition → Evaluation
- Question → Answer

Structure emerges from anchor sequencing.

Structure is not imposed.
 It is observed.

------

# Critical Distinctions

Clause-level ≠ Sentence-level ≠ Paragraph-level

Anchor clause ≠ Paragraph thesis
 Subordinate clause ≠ Unimportant

Structure precedes interpretation.

Interpretation must respect established structure.

------

# Diagnostic Check

You have mastered this layer if you can:

- Identify subordinators quickly
- Keep full subordinated units intact
- Identify all anchor clauses
- Recognize coordinated anchors
- Map anchor sequence across a paragraph
- Do this in narrative, argument, wisdom, and epistle

When this is reliable, interpretation becomes disciplined.
