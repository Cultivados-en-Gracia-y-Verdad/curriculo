\# Manual Methodology — 1 Corintios

\## Purpose of the Manual

The purpose of this manual is not merely to repeat the biblical text word-for-word, but to support the reader through careful textual clarification and structured explanation.

A manual exists to:
\- clarify,
\- organize,
\- explain,
\- connect ideas,
\- and help the reader follow the flow of the text.

If the manual only repeated the biblical text mechanically, the manual itself would become unnecessary.

Therefore, explanation itself is not the problem.

The goal is not:
\> “Say nothing beyond the text.”

The goal is:
\> “Clarify without importing foreign ideas.”

\---

\# Editorial Philosophy

The manual seeks to remain:
\- text-bound,
\- grammatically aware,
\- structurally sensitive,
\- and pedagogically useful.

This means the manual may:
\- paraphrase,
\- clarify,
\- simplify difficult wording,
\- explain grammatical implications,
\- explain structural flow,
\- restate ideas for clarity,
\- and make explicit implications naturally arising from the wording or context of the passage.

However, the manual seeks to avoid:
\- imported theological systems,
\- speculative conclusions,
\- doctrinal overstatements,
\- foreign categories imposed onto the text,
\- or conclusions that extend beyond what the text reasonably supports.

\---

\# Clarification vs Imposition

The distinction is not:

| Incorrect Standard |
|---|
| “Text” vs “anything beyond the text” |

Such a standard would eliminate teaching itself.

Rather, the distinction is:

| Correct Standard |
|---|
| Text-bound clarification vs foreign imposition |

The guiding editorial question is:

\> “Can this clarification reasonably arise from the wording, grammar, or flow of the text itself?”

If yes, then the clarification is considered legitimate manual explanation.

If no, then the statement may be introducing ideas foreign to the passage.

\---

\# Examples

\## Legitimate Clarification

Text:
\> “Grace was given to you.”

Legitimate clarification:
\> “Grace is described as something received rather than achieved.”

This clarification arises naturally from:
\- the passive wording,
\- the concept of something “given,”
\- and the flow of the sentence itself.

This is considered acceptable explanatory clarification.

\---

\## Example of Drift

Text:
\> “Grace was given to you.”

Potential drift:
\> “This proves an entire theological system concerning positional sanctification.”

This moves beyond the reasonable scope of the passage itself and introduces external theological formulation.

\---

\# Editorial Standard for Version 0.3

The goal of Version 0.3 is not sterile mechanical commentary.

The goal is:
\> disciplined textual explanation.

Therefore, Version 0.3 seeks to:
\- preserve explanation,
\- preserve pedagogy,
\- preserve clarity,
\- while increasing:
  \- precision,
  \- textual restraint,
  \- grammatical accuracy,
  \- and reproducibility.

\---

\# Allowed in the Manual

The following are considered acceptable:

\- paraphrase,
\- clarification,
\- explicit implications,
\- grammatical implications,
\- structural explanation,
\- discourse-flow explanation,
\- pedagogical restatement,
\- simplification of difficult ideas,
\- contextual continuity observations.

\---

\# Avoided in the Manual

The following are intentionally avoided:

\- imported theological systems,
\- speculative motives,
\- unsupported doctrinal certainty,
\- universalized claims beyond the scope of the passage,
\- foreign conceptual grids,
\- conclusions stronger than the textual evidence,
\- theological synthesis presented as if directly stated by the verse.

\---

\# Final Guiding Principle

The governing principle for the manual is not:

\> “Never say more than the text.”

Rather:

\> “Never say more than the text reasonably supports.”
